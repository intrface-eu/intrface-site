## Overall Impression

This is a good site with a bad front door and two broken details on every page.

The deep pages are genuinely excellent — `/en/method` and `/en/work/polis` do something almost no consultancy site does, which is make the *absence* of shipped product into the argument. The type system is load-bearing engineering: ten roles each carrying its own measure in `ch`, hyphenation scoped to display and heading below `64rem`, with the result that German is 4% taller than English with zero overflow. Every committed color pair passes AA with margin. `FadeIn` is the correct way to do scroll reveals and most sites get it wrong.

Then the last thing on every page is a wordmark whose letters render as overlapping construction rectangles, the first five tab stops have no focus ring, and the page that has to answer "who am I hiring" never names a person.

**The single biggest opportunity:** the site's peak is three clicks deep. `/en/work/polis` proves more with one diagram and zero screenshots than the home page does with three screenshots nobody can read. Move that register to the front door and the score moves ten points.

## What's Working

**The honesty architecture is a design system, not a disclaimer.** *"Pre-launch means pre-launch. Nothing here claims users it does not have."* *"They go up here once the clients have them in hand, and not before."* *"Hashes here are illustrative."* It works because it converts the weakness (nothing launched) into the proposition (you can check everything we say), and because a competitor cannot paste it — it is about your own gaps.

**The type and i18n implementation.** Ten roles in `globals.css:217-350`, measure on the role in `ch` so no component has to remember it, hyphenation scoped by width and role. Measured: German home 6,889px vs English 6,605px, zero horizontal overflow at 390px, the longest German label at 134px against English's 95px and still fitting. A `hr-HR` browser gets a 307 to `/hr` and the Croatian reads natively.

**`FadeIn`.** Hidden state set from an effect and never on the server, so no FOUC and no hydration mismatch; readable with JS off; one module-level shared observer instead of N; reduced-motion handled purely in CSS so the hidden state cannot exist for a reader who asked for less.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] Nobody is home at the contact moment

**What.** The product truth is one person in Istria. The site says "we" throughout, names no one, shows no face, no CV, no registered company name or OIB outside `/imprint`, and no response-time commitment. The home contact band's left column is structurally empty: `home-page.tsx:335` uses `lg:grid-cols-[1fr_1fr] lg:items-start`, the left column's content ends at y≈390 and the band runs to y≈1000 — roughly 500px of blank ink beside a 690px form. The `/about` version of the same band *does* carry `WHAT HELPS` and the best reassurance line on the site, *"You get a person, not a sales sequence."*

On top of that the form's designed error layer is unreachable: `contact-form.tsx:172` sets `noValidate={false}` while fields carry `required`, so Chrome's native bubble fires first (verified live: empty submit yields `validationMessage: "Please fill out this field."`, `errShown: false`). The translated error copy in all four `messages/*.json`, the per-field `aria-invalid`, `--form-danger` and `focusFirstError` are dead code for the common case — and the native message follows the *browser's* locale, so a Croatian page can throw an English error. The input borders measure **1.27:1** against their white fill, failing WCAG 1.4.11's 3:1 for UI boundaries. The submit uses `tactileButtonClasses("primary")` directly instead of `TactileButton`, so the site's most important click has no hover and no press feedback while the decorative hero anchors get `whileHover={{y:-2, scale:1.015}}`.

**Why it matters.** For an Istrian SME owner or a municipal buyer, "who am I hiring, and is this one person or a team" is the deciding question. Answering it with 500px of empty dark blue, no name, and no data-use line reads as hiding the scale — the opposite of the honesty the rest of the site earns. And the one moment where a visitor commits is the one moment with no designed feedback.

**Fix.** Port the `/about` left column into `home-page.tsx:336-363` verbatim and add three lines: who you are by name, a response-time commitment, one sentence on what happens to the data. Put the registered name and OIB in `fact-ledger.tsx` next to `COMPANY` and `BASED`. Set `noValidate` on the form and keep validation server-side. Cut the topic set from six to four, drop the preselection, add `?topic=` so `Send us your profile` lands with the right chip. Raise the input border to a ≥3:1 token. Route the submit through `TactileButton`.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`, then `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] Two rendering defects in the chrome every page carries

**What.** `globals.css:924-929` draws the outlined footer `INTRFACE` with `-webkit-text-stroke` over `color: transparent`, which strokes every contour including the glyph's internal construction: the T and F crossbars render as closed rectangles crossing the stem, the R's bowl overlaps its leg, the A's bar is a trapezoid whose corners miss the diagonals. It is 64px tall on mobile, ~200px on desktop, the last thing on every page, and it destroys the documented "unprinted plate" concept. Separately, `site-nav.tsx:32` and the brand link at `header.tsx:25` carry no `focus-visible` styles, so the first five tab stops fall back to the UA default — and because `SiteNav` renders an `after:` accent underline at `-bottom-1.5`, the UA outline wraps the pseudo-element too, drawing a box around the word plus a separate box around the underline. 17 of 47 tab stops lack the site's own ink ring, and there is no skip link.

**Why it matters.** Both live on 100% of pageviews, in the two elements that carry the brand. A visitor deciding whether you can be trusted with their software is looking for exactly this class of slip.

**Fix.** Replace the stroke approach with an inline SVG whose letter paths are unioned once at export, keeping the per-letter `clip-path` choreography on `<path>` elements — or cheaper, layer a solid ink copy under a paper-filled copy inset by the stroke width. Add `focus-visible:outline-2 focus-visible:outline-offset-4 focus-visible:outline-ink` to `site-nav.tsx:32` and `header.tsx:25`, and a skip link in `app/[locale]/layout.tsx`.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish`

### [P1] One composition, nine dead columns

**What.** The dominant layout on every page is *label + heading + lead on the left, content on the right, left column bottoming out at 40% of the band's height*. It is literally the same grid twice on the home page — `lg:grid-cols-[.85fr_1.15fr] lg:items-start` at `home-page.tsx:167` and `:290` — plus `[1fr_1fr]` at `:335` and `[.8fr_1.2fr]` in `system-ledger.tsx:36`, where the *wide* column holds a 25px title and a pill while the *narrow* one carries everything. `/en/work/polis`'s best content runs four hairline rows across the full 1200px shell with text filling 580px: 620px of nothing for 900px of scroll. The hero repeats it — `lg:grid-cols-[1fr_auto]` at `home-page.tsx:134` strands the CTA pair ~350px right of the sentence that motivates it, and 390px on `/hr` where the headline is shorter.

**Why it matters.** The page-to-page impression becomes "I have seen this band before", which reads as a template even though the content varies more than almost any consultancy site. And the hero's most important click is spatially divorced from its reason.

**Fix.** Hero: collapse to `lg:grid-cols-1` and put the buttons directly under the lead at `mt-8` — the halftone then gets the whole right side and the CTA sits where the eye already is. Ledgers: invert the ratios (`system-ledger.tsx:36` → `lg:grid-cols-[.35fr_1fr]`) and either fill the second column of the Polis rows with the `WHY IT MATTERS` clause as a right-hand marginal note, or narrow the hairline container so the rule stops advertising a column that does not exist.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

### [P2] The front door is the least authored surface

**What.** `thesis.title` = *"Software is becoming the interface to everything."* — any agency ships this. `hero.seeWork` = "See the work" and `hero.talk` = "Talk to us" are the two most generic CTA labels in existence, and they violate DESIGN.md's own content rule while the deep pages obey it (`Discuss trust infrastructure`, `Bring us the messy system`). Mono is 1.2% of the home page's text against 26% on `/en/work/polis`, which is 11 points over the contract's 15% ceiling. The hero's decorative anchor is the halftone wash — the most category-generic element in the build, occupying 35% of the first viewport with its rationale living only in DESIGN.md. And the hero lead front-loads absence: *"One live client site in Vrsar. Voyager, Polis and Funda, still pre-launch. Agent Ops Cockpit, public under Apache-2.0."* One thing exists, three don't, here's a licence — and nothing says what the visitor gets.

**Why it matters.** `/en/method` and `/en/work/polis` would win a technical buyer. Most visitors decide on `/en` and never reach them.

**Fix.** Rewrite `thesis.title` into a claim only INTRFACE can make — the ledger content is already there (*"Every claim in this system resolves to a hash, a seal, and a timestamp"*). Replace both hero CTAs with the doctrine-compliant verbs the deep pages already use. Move one mono artifact into the first viewport. Rebalance the mono budget in both directions. And either give the halftone a job — mask it to something that means something, e.g. the four-file contract — or cut it and let the type carry the hero.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`, then `/impeccable typeset`

### [P2] The proof cannot be read

**What.** The whole argument is *look at the real interfaces*, and every screenshot is rendered too small to read. The Velum desktop capture is a 1440px page shown at 546px (342px on mobile) — body text lands around 5px. On `/en/method`, the band labelled `EVIDENCE — What it looks like while it runs`, with the copy *"No mock-ups — this is the terminal we work in"*, presents three terminal captures at 1300px and 620px in which not one word is legible. The assets themselves are fine: `naturalWidth` 384–544, correct `srcset`/`sizes`, and `_next/image` serves the 621KB Velum PNG as an 8.4KB WebP at `w=384`. This is a composition decision, not a broken pipeline. Compounding it: there is **no `og:image` and no `twitter:image` on any route**, so `twitter:card` falls back to `summary` and every share of this site renders as a text-only card.

**Why it matters.** An illegible screenshot is a texture, and a texture proves nothing. Meanwhile `/en/work/polis` — which has no screenshots and says so — proves far more with a diagram.

**Fix.** For each proof plate, ship a *cropped detail* at readable scale as the hero image and demote the full page to a small context inset: for Velum, the language switcher and the `LocalBusiness` JSON-LD block; for AOC, the agent pane's rules list; for the task ledger, four rows at 1:1. `evidence-frame.tsx` already owns the frame — add a `crop`/`detail` variant rather than scaling whole pages down. Ship an `og:image`.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

## Minor Observations

- **Fonts are the heaviest thing on the site, not images.** `/en` transfers 618.6 KiB across 46 requests; three preloaded woff2 files total 200,144 B — **32% of the page**, the largest single at 120,112 B. All images together are 44,888 B.
- **20 RSC prefetch requests on one home-page view**, 57,711 B — more than every image on the page combined, and the same routes refetched under different `_rsc` hashes (`/en/about` ×5, `/en/work/voyager` ×5, `/en/method` ×4).
- 4 of 7 meta descriptions exceed the ~155–160 char SERP cut: `/de` 187, `/en/work/polis` 183, `/en` 179, `/en/method` 170. Titles are all fine.
- `/` → `/en` is a **307**, not a 308/301, so search engines will not consolidate signals to `/en` as firmly.
- `numbered-section-labels` fires ×4 on `/en/work/voyager` (`01`–`04`). Those four items are independent engineering facts, not a sequence — numbering that encodes nothing.
- `trust-pipeline-diagram.tsx:79` places the mono artifact chip at `mt-4` after variable-length copy with no `mt-auto`, so the six chips sit at four different heights. Same file uses `text-[0.8rem]` and `text-[0.85rem]`, two sizes not in the scale, where `.type-data` belongs.
- `home-page.tsx:187-199`: the thesis 3-up gives cell 1 `sm:first:pl-0` and cell 3 `sm:last:pr-0` while cell 2 keeps its 24px inset, so `Implementation` runs flush to the shell edge with no right gutter while its siblings are inset.
- **`.type-heading` is weight 550 and `.type-subheading` is 600 — the weight step runs backwards down the scale.** That is why the ink ledger squints as four peers rather than a heading plus three names.
- `100svh` hero plus `my-auto` centring means shorter locales get *more* dead space, not less.
- `Open source · AGPL · pre-deployment` is a three-fact sentence in a 284px status pill beside `Pre-launch` (95px) and `Active build` (108px) — three status vocabularies, so the reader cannot rank the three systems by readiness.
- The header carries no CTA at all: four muted text links plus a raw native `<select>` that is the visually heaviest element in the bar and the one un-designed control on the site.
- The footer wordmark measures 1,205px inside a 1,200px container — 5px from setting a scrollbar, despite a code comment claiming it never can.
- The primary button's hover lift keeps `shadow-xl` unchanged, so it rises with no depth cue — a jitter rather than a lift, and `scale` on pill text risks a soft re-raster mid-transition.
- DESIGN.md is stale in one place: it says the header *"collapses nav into a details-based menu below `lg`"*. It does not — `mobile-nav.tsx` is a React-state `AnimatePresence` panel (confirmed against the live HTML: zero `<details>`).

## Questions to Consider

1. **What if the home page had no hero decoration and no thesis band — just the claim, then `/en/work`'s numbered ledger?** The `/work` index and the `/method` hero are the two best compositions on this site and neither uses the halftone. If the strongest surfaces don't need it, what is it for?
2. **You sell "one person, radically legible."** So why is the one thing the site never makes legible *the person*? What changes if viewport one on `/about` is a name, a face, a location, and a sentence saying exactly what one engineer can and cannot take on?
3. **`365,000 lines of TypeScript` and `55 Postgres tables` are the loudest numbers on the site, and neither is a claim a buyer can act on.** What are the three numbers a Vrsar restaurant owner and a Croatian municipality would actually weigh — days from brief to live URL, languages a guest can ask in, days a month you're available — and what happens to the ink ledger if you swap them in?
4. **`/en/work/polis` proves more with a diagram and zero screenshots than the rest of the site does with six screenshots.** Its own copy says why. So why does the home page lead with three unreadable screenshots instead of three diagrams of what each one does?
5. **Three pages render "four numbered steps" with the identical `ProcessSteps` component while the same idea gets five different treatments elsewhere.** The macro structure repeats and the components diverge — exactly backwards. What if each *page* had a distinct form (index / document / diagram / dossier) and each *idea* had exactly one component?
